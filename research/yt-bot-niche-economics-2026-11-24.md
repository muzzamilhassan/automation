# YT-BOT niche: economics, policy and demand — nine directions compared (2026-11-24)

All sources read **2026-11-24** unless a row says otherwise. Read-only research; no code changed.

**How pages were read.** Pages were opened with a fetch tool that passes the page through a small model and limits quotes to short fragments. Text in quotation marks is what that tool returned as verbatim. Before quoting anything externally, re-read the live page.

**Labels.** FIRST-PARTY = the platform/programme/company's own page. DATASET = third-party with stated method and sample. PRESS = named news outlet. CREATOR = self-reported. WEAK = marketing blog, vendor, or modelled number. OUR INFERENCE = our reasoning, not a source. "not found" = searched, nothing usable.

**Not in this report.** No winner is picked. Channel-performance evidence (yt-dlp) is being measured separately.

---

## Summary

1. **No trustworthy RPM exists for eight of the nine directions.** The only dataset with a stated method (AIR, 300 channels) has no "AI", "programming", "cybersecurity", "productivity" or "business documentary" category. It measures Education & Science at $10.22 median and Gadgets & Tech at $2.33, and the Education figure has its own problem (A.1 note). Which bucket our channel would land in is unknown; that 4x gap is bigger than most blog-claimed niche gaps.
2. **Timing matters more than niche.** From 1 Feb 2027 new channels need 1,000 subs plus **8,000** hours/365 days or **20M** Shorts views/90 days. That is 69 days away; a channel that is empty today will almost certainly face the doubled bar. [S1]
3. **Pakistan gets the full YPP but nothing else.** Not on the list for: the 500-sub early tier, channel memberships, Super Thanks, BrandConnect/Creator Partnerships, or the Shopping affiliate programme. All five checked first-party. [S3–S7]
4. **A termination is owner-wide, not channel-wide** (first-party [S16b]): it covers all existing channels. A risky new niche is a risk to the whole portfolio.
5. **The "AI affiliate" story is thinner than it looks.** Anthropic's referral reward is credit with "no cash value"; no OpenAI cash programme found; Notion is closed. Programmes that do pay mostly use PayPal (not available in Pakistan), PartnerStack or Wise.
6. **Policy exposure is mostly about production method, not topic.** The clause that fits us is "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". Topic shifts the odds: tutorials were named by YouTube's T&S chief; finance/legal/health/politics trigger the AI-persona rule; even Kurzgesagt was falsely flagged.
7. **Demand for learning AI is real; sentiment is turning.** Coursera+Udemy 45+ GenAI enrolments/minute (25 in 2025). But US CS enrolment −12.4%, 52% of Americans more concerned than excited, comfort with fully AI-made news about 12%.
8. **Evergreen:** 3, 4, 7 strongest; 1 and 2 weakest (OUR INFERENCE — no reputable half-life study found).

## C) Platform and accuracy risk

### C.1 Rules (FIRST-PARTY)

- **Inauthentic/generic** [S14]: content must "Not be mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative". Not monetisable: content that "looks like it's made with a template"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"; image slideshows, templated storylines, scrolling text with little narrative or educational value. Allowed: same intro/outro with different substance; series with a distinct focus per video. Enforced at channel level.
- **AI personas on sensitive topics** [S14]: "channels that use AI-generated personas to deliver information on sensitive topics" — health, legal, finance, politics — cannot monetise; example, an AI podcast host giving financial guidance. Tubefilter [S15] reports the trigger as content presenting itself as a human expert giving advice. Whether a disembodied TTS narrator is a "persona" is **not defined**.
- **Spam** [S16]: bans "Using automated tools or AI to churn out high volumes of similar content with minimal changes"; covers "coordinated networks of channels". Penalty up to termination.
- **Termination is owner-wide** [S16b]: after one, "you are prohibited from using, possessing, or creating any other YouTube channels"; covers existing channels and channels where you are repeatedly or prominently featured.
- **Disclosure** [S17][S18]: realistic altered/synthetic content must be disclosed; AI script help and clearly non-realistic animation need not be. Cloning *your own* voice is exempt; **the page does not state the same for a generic TTS voice** (our 2026-10-24 file reads narration as exempt; treat as probable, not explicit). Since May 2026 YouTube auto-labels significant *photorealistic* AI; a label "alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it's eligible to earn money". Diagrams and white-page notes are the lowest-exposure visuals. A cloned voice of a third person, or a realistic AI scene of a named CEO (direction 6), is not.
- **Hacking** [S19][S19b]: no teaching credential theft or unauthorised access; penetration testing, bug bounties, VPN and documentary coverage are ad-eligible.

## E) The case against

**Against "for AI" specifically**
1. AI is where AI-made video is most expected and most copied. The obvious topics (what is an LLM, agents, RAG) have strong incumbents — *the size of that gap is the yt-dlp question* (OUR INFERENCE).
2. The best-monetised AI format (2) needs screen recordings and hands-on use. We have neither; describing tools we did not operate is close to the "reproducing" pattern Halprin cited.
3. AI affiliate income is weak where it should be strongest: no cash from Claude; none found for ChatGPT; n8n is PayPal-only; Make is Wise-only.
4. No RPM evidence says an AI channel earns Education-level rates.
5. Sentiment: 52% more concerned than excited, under-30s now a majority [S69]; sponsored-AI backlash [S48].
6. Substitution: 38% used genAI to get information in a week [S62]; NotebookLM makes explainer videos (press extracts); Ask YouTube gives structured answers (US Premium 18+ at launch; search extracts, reach UNVERIFIED). Generic explainers are the most replaceable content.
7. An AI-voiced channel about AI invites "slop" comments. Size of any viewer penalty for AI narration: not found (vendor claims only).
8. Hype decay (crypto analogue).

**Against entering at all now**
1. **Doubled threshold in 69 days**, with no Pakistan early tier underneath. Income before 8,000 hours must come from off-platform affiliate/product/sponsor routes.
2. **Portfolio risk.** Spam policy names coordinated networks; January reports single out 10+-channel operators; a termination covers all the owner's channels [S16b]. Shared renderer and voice is the visible fingerprint.
3. Existing channels already sit in a sensitive category (finance). Script-approval time is the scarce resource; fixing those may return more (decide with own data).
4. Pakistan is the country most cited for trending slop channels [S71]. Whether location affects enforcement: no evidence found.
5. No evidence, either way, of small new faceless neural-voice channels reaching YPP in these niches in 2026.

**What would reduce risk whichever direction wins (OUR INFERENCE)**
- Distinct concept and diagram logic per video; a visible original artefact (worked example, code that ran, our own benchmark, sources on screen).
- No persona, no first-person expertise claims, no finance/legal/health/politics advice.
- Distinct voice and identity from other channels; low cadence; keep approval records as appeal evidence.
- Treat 1 and 2 as occasional, not core; 5 only as reported evidence, not advice.
